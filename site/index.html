Open meeting

Four reasons it speaks up. Otherwise, silence.

Every twenty seconds CASE reads the last three minutes of the call and asks the model one question: is there a reason to speak up right now? Most of the time the answer is no, and you hear nothing. When it is yes, you get a beep and a card. Nobody else does.

Transcript lines and names on this page are fictional stand-ins. The four kinds, the timings, and the card are the real product.

It never joins the call, and nothing leaves your machine.

Both sides, transcribed locally

Your microphone and the system audio of everyone else run through local speech models on your computer. Each line is tagged as you or them. There is no bot in the participant list.

Your model, your choice

Point the assistant at a local model through Ollama or LM Studio and nothing leaves the machine. Use a cloud provider with your own key if you prefer. One provider runs the observer, summaries, and vision.

Only you hear it

The beep plays on your side. The card floats above the call on your screen. CASE never speaks into the call. Whether to say the thing out loud stays your decision.

A meeting is a thread, not a file.

End a meeting today and continue it tomorrow. Each session ends with a short summary that merges into the thread’s rolling summary, so the assistant walks into the next call already knowing what was decided.

Meetings live in projects. A project holds a brief and sources: pasted notes, local files and folders, checked-out repos, links, images. The assistant starts each check with only the brief and the last summary, and reads sources on its own when the conversation calls for it.

⌘⇧M

One shortcut runs the whole loop.

Press it once to start a meeting, once more to end it. The meeting starts unfiled; the assistant files it into a project once it knows what it is about. The tray menu, the command line, and a Unix signal do the same thing, so it fits whatever starts your calls.

Twenty seconds, start to card.
